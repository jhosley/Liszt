# 08 · What Liszt does not do

A program earns trust by being plain about its edges. These six boundaries are
deliberate, and holding them is what keeps the record small, portable and true.

**It does not replace anyone's judgment.** The analyst still decides what the
chain is, the data owner still decides what a score should be, and leadership
still decides the priority. Liszt records those judgments so they can be
checked, compared and handed on.

**It does not own the telemetry.** The systems that produce evidence, the
endpoint agents, the audit logs, the platform pipelines, stay exactly where
they are. The record says only what they emit, whether we would see it, and
who owns the gap.

**It does not own operational performance.** The operator platform stays the
system of record for what fired and how it was resolved. Liszt holds the
specification and the authorization: what should be detected, what a use case
is permitted to do, and the evidence that justified that permission on a given
date.

**It does not decide the tooling.** A record produces the requirement: a named
signal, a named gap, a named owner. The owner with the budget decides how to
meet it, by hauling the data back, by having the platform emit it, or by
accepting the gap, and that decision gets recorded instead of made by default.

**It does not become a second operations function.** Monitoring, triage and
response stay where they are today. Liszt hands them requirements and receives
their results, and that exchange is the whole relationship.

**It does not replace the frameworks.** It uses them at pinned versions, so a
number means the same thing at both ends of a year. Where a mapping between
frameworks is the program's own judgment, the record says so plainly rather
than implying an endorsement nobody gave.
