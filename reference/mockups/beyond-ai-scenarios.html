STEP TWO

Say what it runs on

This is the part that does not exist today. Liszt currently has one environment, the AI stack, so nobody has to name it. The moment there is more than one, every proposal has to stand somewhere.

Why a catalog rather than free text. "A web app behind an API gateway" is a shape our estate takes many times over. Describe it once, carefully, with the people who own it, and every scenario against it inherits the layers, the owners and the log sources for free.
An open design question, worth further discussion. The two endpoint entries below are not shapes, they are conditions on a population. "Hosts missing EDR" describes a state, not an architecture. Either they are environments with a filter attached, or Liszt needs a second idea alongside environment. This mockup treats them as environments so the seam is visible. The likely answer is that a shape and a condition are two different things, and the note on the engineering sheet sets out why.

What an engineer fills in once, with the people who own the platform. Every scenario against this shape then inherits the layers, the components, the log sources and the owners without anyone retyping them.

How to read it. A component is a thing that exists and can be pointed at. What it emits is what a scenario step landing on that layer would have to work with. A layer with nothing in the emits column is a blind layer before anyone scores a single scenario.

STEP THREE

Run the research

Liszt picks the prompts from the library that match this environment and runs them. These are the same prompts anybody can read, edit, version and schedule.

STEP FOUR

Review before anything is written

Everything below is a proposal from the research, not a decision. The engineer corrects the chain, chooses the framework tags, and only then does it go to the converter.

PROPOSED ATTACK PATH

Layer tags come from the environment's own vocabulary, so a reviewer picks from a list rather than inventing a string. This is what stops the tagging drift we have been fighting.

STEP FIVE

The converter runs on a reviewed answer

By this point the layer, the chain and the framework tags have all been chosen by a person from a closed list. The converter is doing a mechanical translation rather than a judgement call, which is the whole reason to put a review step in front of it.

ValidationAll step tags are in the environment vocabulary. The chosen layer appears in the chain. Every framework identifier is well formed. Source tiers are 1, 2 or 3.
